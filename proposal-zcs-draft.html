Overview

A clearer welcome for new families. Less work for your team.

You described a website that works, but has grown “arms and legs” as new pages and requests have been added. It’s “due for a cleanup.” With open house and enrollment season approaching, the first priority is helping prospective families find information, ask a question, and take the next step.

What I heard you want

  • A simpler admissions experience. Clear, consistent steps to inquire, visit, and apply.
  • A refresh of the WordPress site you already have. Better navigation, reliable layouts on phones and tablets, and more room for the school’s story.
  • Help with search visibility. Make the programs and information families need easier to find through Google and AI search tools.
  • A partner for design and technical support. Your team can keep handling everyday content changes, with someone available for the bigger questions.
  • A choice of scope and commitment. Project options, plus six-month and one-year care plans.

Your three-year vision, as I heard it: technical support is no longer a headache, the website stays fresh, ZCS keeps pace with changes in search, and the prospective family experience is “second to none.” You want the site to become an example other schools can learn from.

Tell the story families should see.

Your history, Christian education, Spanish and Mandarin immersion, and outdoor learning offer plenty to talk about. The homepage can give families a clearer picture of those experiences, using the photos, videos, and language your team already creates.

Help families find their next step.

Prospective families come first, with clear routes for current families and donors too. Consistent buttons and helpful explanations can make it easier to understand what happens before an inquiry, tour, or application.

Measure what changes.

You planned to ask your admissions director about inquiry and tour numbers. I’ll review what’s already tracked, then help connect website activity with those records where the admissions tools allow it. That gives us a starting point for judging improvements, rather than guessing at enrollment results.

Initial findings

What came up in our conversation.

These observations come from our meeting walkthrough and your team’s experience. I haven’t re-tested them as part of this proposal review. I’ll verify each one, agree on the intended behavior with your team, and confirm where the change needs to happen.

1Homepage video on mobile. You mentioned during our conversation that the homepage video doesn’t always load on phones. I’ll check playback on mobile and review the video settings and delivery with your current host if needed, before recommending a fix.

2Admissions buttons. The walkthrough raised inconsistent destinations across Inquire, Visit, and Apply. Inquire opened a form in some places and a portal sign-in on the homepage; Apply also led to sign-in. I’ll map all three routes and confirm when an account is actually needed.

3English / Traditional program link. The English link under Explore appeared to open the outdoor program. We found a separate Traditional page during the call. I’ll check whether the link has since been corrected and confirm the right label and destination.

4Footer social links. Several links appeared broken or led somewhere unexpected. I’ll check each one against ZCS’s current social accounts.

The rest of the first review

  • Clickable contact email addresses. The walkthrough found email text that had to be copied and pasted. I’ll check the contact page and make the addresses open an email app with a click.
  • A lighter first inquiry. Forms reviewed during the call asked for a child’s birthday, current school, and mailing address. I’ll ask admissions which details are needed at first contact and which can wait.
  • A clearer tour scheduling process. The tour request didn’t make it clear how to choose a time. I’ll confirm whether families should select an available slot, suggest a time, or wait for staff to arrange the visit, then make those instructions clear.
  • Yellow and white color combinations. Contrast concerns came up during the walkthrough. I’ll check text and buttons for accessibility and adjust the combinations that are difficult to read while keeping the ZCS identity.
  • Mobile and tablet formatting. You mentioned differences after desktop edits, and we discussed shifting buttons and the About page’s core values. I’ll test those areas at phone, tablet, and desktop sizes and address the confirmed layout issues.

Changes within WordPress

Link destinations, clickable email addresses, button labels, color combinations, and page formatting can usually be adjusted in the existing site. I’ll verify the cause before making changes.

Changes that need another system

Inquiry fields, tour availability, and account requirements may be controlled by an external admissions system. Reviewing the options and updating website links and instructions are included. Changes inside that system depend on its settings, access, and provider support; any extra work or cost will be agreed first.

What I’d keep

The navigation that stays visible as you scroll, clear tuition information, your school history, and your team’s photography and videos are useful foundations. I’d build on those strengths while keeping the path for new families clear.

What I’ll do

Start with the essentials. Build from there.

Give the school’s story more room.

Make useful information easier to find.

Understand what’s working.

I’ll review existing analytics and set up tracking for the website’s admissions buttons and any forms we can measure. Completed inquiries and bookings inside another system depend on the reporting and access it provides.

Choose support after launch.

An optional care plan covers ongoing technical support, search improvements, and small design updates. Your team keeps handling the everyday content changes.

Replacing an admissions system, building a custom connection to it, or paying for provider changes is also outside this quote.
